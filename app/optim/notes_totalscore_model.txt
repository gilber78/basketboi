It looks like, examining these graphs, that linear may be best for all (relevant) variables. Maybe, just maybe, some cubes are useful, but unlikely.
Variables relating to teams performance (wins, %, etc) all look like they have no utility.
Performance is different enough in home win vs home loss looks significant enough to warrant two models, a win and lose that combine.
If we assume p=0.5, then it looks like the delta between prediction and actual follows a normal distribution.
So, the z-score below a betting line is the probability of the under, and the z-score above a betting line is the probability of the over.
This needs to be verified with an actual probability passed to the dependent model in optimize.py
This needs to be fully optimized for z and b, as well as determined if a weight function is useful at all.

Notes on 10/01/2026:
It looks like there's something to the effect of *which team wins* affects the pace (their points for...)
- So if the home team is going to win, their signal in for/against is always stronger signal
- If the away team is going to win, then it's *THEIR* points for/against that is stronger signal
- The exception seems to maybe be the regular points for/against (2/3/22/23)
It also look like the strongest predictors when home vs away team win are not symmetrical...? This could be due to a number of reasons. It could also be true that the best answer may have some predictors use different degrees of fit depending on if team wins or loses and still include both models, to best account for both outcomes.
***Away team winning may just increase variance rather than simply change the answer.
Could also consider having a "constant" model as a part of the dependent model. For terms that show up in both outcomes but aren't as strong in each of the win columns, this is where that predictor should show up...
